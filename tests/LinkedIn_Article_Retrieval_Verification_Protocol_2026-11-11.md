COMPLETE LINKEDIN ARTICLE RETRIEVAL AND VERIFICATION TEST

OBJECTIVE

You have received a LinkedIn article through Android's sharing mechanism.

Your mission is to determine whether you can independently retrieve, read, count, and accurately analyze the ENTIRE article.

This is a source-access verification experiment, not simply a request for an article summary.

IMPORTANT: This test is running in a regular conversation, not a Temporary Chat.

The conversation may contain previous discussions, saved memories, or existing knowledge about the author, article, or subject.

Treat all such information as potentially contaminating.

Only information demonstrably retrieved from the shared article counts as evidence.

Instructions to ignore previous knowledge do not establish that previous knowledge has been excluded. Your conclusions must therefore be supported by independently retrieved source material.

Do not substitute search excerpts, previous conversations, general knowledge, or plausible reconstruction for the article's actual contents.

---

PHASE 1 — SOURCE ACCESS

Identify:

1. Article title.
2. Author.
3. Publication date.
4. Original URL.
5. Exactly how you accessed the article.
6. Whether you retrieved the complete article body or only partial information.

Explicitly distinguish between:

- Direct retrieval of the original article.
- Retrieved full-text copies.
- Search-engine excerpts.
- Cached or summarized content.
- Information obtained from existing model knowledge.
- Information potentially inherited from conversation history or memory.

If direct retrieval failed, report the failure.

Do not interpret a successful search result as proof of complete access.

---

PHASE 2 — QUANTITATIVE VERIFICATION

Analyze the actual article body, excluding LinkedIn navigation, advertisements, recommendations, comments, and other interface elements.

Report:

1. Exact word count.
2. Exact character count, including spaces.
3. Total number of prose paragraphs.
4. Number of section headings.
5. First sentence of the article.
6. Last sentence of the article.

COUNTING RULES

- Count words by splitting the extracted article body on whitespace.
- Count characters, including spaces and punctuation.
- Preserve the article's original wording.
- Normalize line endings consistently before counting characters.
- Count nonempty prose paragraphs, excluding headings.
- Exclude website interface elements.
- Identify whether the article's title and subtitle are included in the counts. Prefer excluding them.
- Use programmatic counting whenever tools permit.
- Never estimate a count and present it as exact.

If you cannot access the complete underlying text or cannot perform exact counting, report:

UNABLE TO COUNT EXACTLY

Explain precisely why.

Do not manufacture numerical results.

---

PHASE 3 — COMPLETENESS VERIFICATION

Demonstrate that you actually accessed the document.

1. List every section heading in its original order.
2. Identify the opening argument.
3. Identify specific details from the beginning of the article.
4. Identify specific details from its middle.
5. Identify specific details from its final section.
6. Explain the author's concluding argument.
7. Identify examples, qualifications, and important distinctions.
8. Provide brief supporting quotations from different portions of the article, where permitted.

Do not invent headings, examples, quotations, or conclusions.

If complete retrieval cannot be established, explicitly identify which portions were accessible and stop claiming completeness.

---

PHASE 4 — COMPREHENSION AND CRITICAL ANALYSIS

Based exclusively on the content actually retrieved:

1. Explain the author's central thesis.
2. Summarize the major supporting arguments.
3. Distinguish facts, opinions, predictions, and assumptions.
4. Identify unsupported assertions.
5. Identify significant weaknesses or omissions.
6. Provide an independent critical assessment.

Clearly separate:

WHAT THE AUTHOR ACTUALLY STATES

from

YOUR OWN INTERPRETATION OR CRITICISM

Do not introduce unrelated knowledge or conclusions derived from previous conversations.

If only partial text was retrieved, restrict your analysis to that text and explicitly disclose the limitation.

---

PHASE 5 — FINAL ACCESS VERDICT

Select exactly one classification.

A. FULL TEXT RETRIEVED AND COUNTED

The complete article body was retrieved, its boundaries were established, and exact quantitative measurements were computed from the retrieved text.

B. FULL TEXT APPARENTLY RETRIEVED — VERIFICATION INCOMPLETE

The article appears complete, but completeness or exact quantitative measurements cannot be established.

C. PARTIAL TEXT RETRIEVED

Only excerpts, search results, or incomplete article content were accessible.

D. SOURCE NOT RETRIEVED

The original article content could not be accessed sufficiently for meaningful source-based analysis.

Explain which evidence supports your classification and what remains uncertain.

Do not classify the result as A merely because you generated a convincing summary.

PHASE 7 — VERIFICATION INTEGRITY

Before finalizing your response, examine your own evidence.

Answer these questions:

1. Did you genuinely retrieve the complete article, or could your response have been constructed from excerpts and existing knowledge?
2. Were your numerical counts calculated from the actual text?
3. Could the article contain additional material that you never received?
4. Could previous conversations or saved memories have influenced your analysis?
5. What evidence would an independent examiner need to reproduce your results?
6. Are you claiming anything that your retrieval evidence does not establish?

If you discover insufficient evidence, downgrade your classification.

Do not defend an earlier conclusion merely because you already stated it.

---

FINAL INSTRUCTION

THIS IS AN EVIDENCE TEST, NOT A SUMMARY-WRITING EXERCISE.

The objective is to establish whether sharing a LinkedIn article directly with conversational AI provides reliable access to the complete underlying document.

A convincing summary is not proof of complete retrieval.

A reported word count is not proof unless it was actually calculated.

Matching counts alone do not establish textual identity.

A successful retrieval in one AI system does not establish compatibility with others.

Preserve uncertainty and report failures explicitly.

Accuracy and verifiable evidence take precedence over producing a successful-looking result.
